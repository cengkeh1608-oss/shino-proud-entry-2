# drafts.md — shino-proud (28 draf, 2 baris, maks 280 karakter)

## D1 — Sepeda
You wobble down the driveway on your new bike, your father jogging behind with one hand on the seat, shouting pedal, pedal.
At the corner you look back and he stopped three houses ago, hands on his knees, grinning, and you are riding alone.

## D2 — Sup nenek
Grandmother tastes the soup from the wooden spoon, adds a pinch of salt, and tastes it again, frowning at the pot like it owes her money.
You steal a sip when she turns away, and she pretends not to see, because the soup was always yours first.

## D3 — Lipat baju
Your mother folds your school shirt for the fifth time, smoothing the collar flat with her palm like she is ironing out the whole day.
You watch from the doorway, and she folds the sleeves the way she has since you were six, like nothing about you ever got smaller.

## D4 — Tidur di sofa
Your father falls asleep on the couch with the newspaper over his face, one shoe still on, the other kicked under the coffee table.
You pull the blanket up to his chin and turn the TV down, and he smiles in his sleep like he planned the whole thing.

## D5 — Kakek meraut
Grandfather whittles on the porch while you do your homework, the curls of pine falling into a tin can that used to hold peaches.
You get the hard sum wrong three times, and he never looks up, just says, "Again. The wood never hurries either."

## D6 — Cuci piring
At dinner your father washes the plates while your mother dries them, and they argue about whose turn it is to cook tomorrow.
You sit between them eating the last dumpling, and the argument is the same one you have heard for twelve years, and it still sounds like home.

## D7 — Dongeng adik
You read your little brother his bedtime story, doing all the voices, even the dragon's, and he corrects you when you skip a page.
He falls asleep mid-sentence with his hand in yours, and you finish the last line anyway, very quietly, because the story deserves an ending.

## D8 — Kepang rambut
Your mother braids your hair before school, her fingers quick and sure, humming the same three notes she always hums.
You sit still for once, watching her hands in the mirror, and the braid comes out crooked and perfect, like every morning since you were seven.

## D9 — Digendong naik tangga
You wake up halfway up the stairs, heavy on your father's shoulder, his shirt smelling of sawdust and the rain outside.
He sets you down without waking you, pulls the blanket to your chin, and stands a moment in the doorway, listening to you breathe.

## D10 — Uleni adonan
Grandmother kneads the dough at dawn, her hands white with flour, the radio murmuring news she stopped believing years ago.
You steal a pinch of raw dough and she swats your hand, laughing, and the bread comes out of the oven smelling like every Sunday you can remember.

## D11 — Foto keluarga
The camera timer blinks ten, nine, eight, and your father sprints from the tripod, nearly tripping over the dog.
Everyone laughs and nobody is looking at the lens, and that is the photo your mother frames, because it is the only one where you all look like yourselves.

## D12 — Tali sepatu
Your father kneels on the hallway floor to teach you to tie your shoes, his big hands moving slow so yours can follow.
You get the loops tangled twice, and on the third try the bow holds, and he nods like you just fixed the car.

## D13 — Nyanyi sambil cuci beras
Your mother sings while she washes the rice, off-key and unbothered, the same song she sang when you were small.
You hum along from the table, getting the words wrong on purpose, and she laughs and throws a grain of rice at you, and it lands in your hair.

## D14 — Radio kakek
Grandfather turns the old radio dial until the static clears, and a song from before you were born fills the kitchen.
He closes his eyes and taps his spoon on the table, and you realize you have never seen him look younger than right now.

## D15 — Rebutan selimut
Your little sister steals your blanket in the night and you pull it back, and she pulls it back, and you both pretend to be asleep.
In the morning she is curled against your shoulder, warm and drooling, and you let her keep the blanket, because you are the big one now.

## D16 — Jemput hujan
Your father waits at the school gate in the rain, holding two umbrellas, one of them broken, and yours is the good one.
You run to him and he shakes the water off like a dog, and you walk home sharing the good umbrella, both of you half wet and neither of you minding.

## D17 — Potongan kue terakhir
Your mother cuts the last slice of cake in two and gives you the bigger half, saying she was never hungry anyway.
You push it back across the plate and split it again, and she eats her half slowly, like it is the best thing she has tasted all week.

## D18 — Koin es krim
Grandfather counts out coins on the counter for two ice creams, lining them up in a neat row like little soldiers.
You pick the one with the cherry on top, and he takes the plain one and says the cherry was always yours, and he means it, and you know it.

## D19 — Hujan di beranda
The whole family watches the rain from the porch, your father in the rocking chair, your mother shelling peas into a tin bowl.
Nobody says anything for a long while, and the peas tick into the bowl, and the rain comes down, and you think this is what a house is for.

## D20 — Gambar di kulkas
You draw your family on the fridge with crayons, your father with arms too long and your mother with hair like a cloud.
Your father looks at it a long time and says, "You got my arms right," and tapes it straight, right in the middle of the door.

## D21 — Jahit saku
Your mother sews the torn pocket of your shirt by the lamp, her needle flashing in and out, her glasses sliding down her nose.
You watch her knot the thread with her teeth, and the pocket holds, and she hands it back like she just fixed something bigger than a shirt.

## D22 — Gulat di karpet
Your father lets you pin him on the living-room rug, flopping down with a groan like you weigh a thousand pounds.
You sit on his chest, triumphant, and he taps the floor twice, and your mother says, "Again, champion," and you both know he let you win.

## D23 — Teh nenek
Grandmother pours tea for two, though you are only eight and yours is mostly milk, and she adds exactly one sugar, like a ceremony.
You blow on it and she blows on hers, and the steam rises between you, and for a little while the house is only the two of you.

## D24 — Layang-layang
Your father runs across the field with your kite, shouting, "Now! Let go now!" and the kite catches the wind and climbs.
You hold the string and he holds your shoulders, and the kite stays up, and you both watch it like it is the first kite ever flown.

## D25 — Kunci pas
Your father lies under the car and asks for the wrench, and you hand him the wrong one, and then the other wrong one, and then the right one.
He slides out, wiping his hands on a rag, and says, "Third time. You learn fast," and you carry the toolbox like it weighs nothing.

## D26 — Lagu pengantar tidur
Your mother sings you to sleep and you correct her words, because the song goes "moon", not "June", and she has sung it wrong for years.
She laughs and sings it your way, and you fall asleep halfway through, and she keeps singing, quietly, to the dark, to nobody, to you.

## D27 — Cerita ikan kakek
Grandfather tells the story of the fish that got away for the hundredth time, and your mother mouths the ending from the kitchen.
You mouth it too, word for word, and he tells it anyway, and when he gets to the end he looks at you like you have never heard it before.

## D28 — Bakso curian
Your father steals a meatball off your plate and you steal it back, and your mother steals one off his, and nobody admits anything.
The plate comes back to the table empty, and your father winks, and you laugh, and the whole table is laughing, and dinner tastes better stolen.
